## 1. Summary

The researcher reports extracting the pattern that acts as the watermark's key without neural networks or any information only the provider holds, simply by averaging a large number of generated images. Once the pattern can be extracted, the inverse, applying the mark to images not made by AI, can also hold on the same principle. Watermark detection works as a layer for sorting large volumes of content by how likely it is to be AI-generated, but it is no basis for treating "no mark = not AI-generated" or "mark present = generated by that model" in regulatory reporting or litigation. <strong>A mark embedded in an artifact can be stripped or applied once enough of it is observed, so where content came from cannot be known from whether the mark is there, unless the generator's signature is checked before the content is accepted.</strong>

## 2. What got through

- **What got through**: Processing that removes about 91% of the SynthID watermark's energy from Gemini-generated images while keeping image quality almost intact. The method, code, and results were published on GitHub (`aloshdenny/reverse-SynthID`) (Alosh Denny's report).
- **Method**: Observing that SynthID encodes a fixed carrier frequency with constant phase, averaging the noise of 123,000 Gemini-generated images to extract the watermark's frequency pattern (the equivalent of the key), then using a phase-shift attack on that frequency to nullify the mark. No neural networks and no information held only by the provider were used (Denny's account).
- **What reached the receiving side**: In general, what reaches the party receiving an image is the image itself, and an image whose mark was stripped cannot be told from one that never had a mark by whether the mark is there. Once the pattern can be extracted, the inverse, applying the mark to a non-AI image (misattribution), can also hold on the same principle.
- **Not disclosed**: Any case in which a stripped or forged image passed at an actual point of acceptance (moderation, regulatory reporting, litigation). An official statement from Google DeepMind (none confirmed through official channels).

## 3. Pre-execution check

Whatever the route, the condition for telling them apart is checking, before an image is accepted, a signature issued by the party that generated it, separately from the mark inside the image.

- **Who approves**: The party that generated the image (the model provider or generation service). At generation, it shows through a signed manifest that the image was made by a legitimate generator.
- **Where it is checked**: Just before the image is accepted. Before it is handled in moderation, regulatory reporting, or as material in litigation, the generator's signature is checked. An image with no signature, or one that does not match, is treated as "generator not confirmed," and whether it is AI-generated is not decided by the mark alone.
- **What is recorded**: Which image was accepted after a check against which generator's signature. Later, it can be traced what each decision rested on.

Watermark detection has practical value as a layer for sorting AI-generated-ness at scale, and this Brief does not deny that role. But as long as the mark sits in the same signal as the image, a layer that checks the generator separately from the mark is needed. A case where the provenance of data does not travel with its content is [Discord](/critical/briefs/008-discord-scraping/) (publication of messages collected through a public API); a case where the authenticity of input to AI is not checked is [Noroboto](/critical/briefs/005-noroboto-lying-fonts/) (font impersonation misdirecting AI document review).

## 4. What Lemma does

**Lemma places the pre-execution check just before an image is accepted.** It does not detect watermarks or judge AI-generated-ness; before acceptance, it checks whether an image was made by a legitimate generator, against the generator's signature held outside the image.

### Industries and operations to watch

- **Industry · AI**: Model providers and generation services that generate images and release them. Under the EU AI Act's watermarking and transparency requirements, how to show the provenance of AI-generated content becomes an issue.
- **Industry · Media**: News organizations and platforms that handle the authenticity of images they receive, and that use watermarks for labeling, moderation, and initial screening of AI-generated content.

### Current controls vs Lemma

The route demonstrated here is column 1 (an AI-generated image with its watermark removed). Column 2 is the inverse operation, set out as possible on the same principle; whether it was actually carried out has not been disclosed.

| Control | Accepting an AI-generated image with the watermark removed as not AI-generated | Accepting a mark falsely applied to a non-AI image as that model's output | Sorting large volumes of content by how likely it is to be AI-generated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Watermark and detection API (SynthID) | Not effective | Not effective | Partial |
| **Lemma (pre-execution check)** | Partial | Effective | Not relevant |

The watermark and detection API can be used for large-scale initial screening, but because the mark sits in the same signal as the image, neither a removed mark nor a falsely applied one can be told apart by whether the mark is there. Lemma checks the generator's signature held outside the image, so a falsely applied mark does not pass the signature check. An image with its mark removed is treated as having no signature and is no basis for "not AI-generated," but Lemma does not judge whether it is AI-generated.

Note: Lemma's check works only when the generator issues a signature at generation and the receiving side builds the check in before moderation, reporting, or review. Images from generators that issue no signature are outside the check. It assumes the generator manages the signing key outside the artifact; if the key leaks, content can pass. The table compares what each control checks; it does not show whether this incident would have been stopped. Lemma does not detect or prevent intrusions.

### Checklist

Three things to confirm in organizations that handle AI-generated content

- Have you set out that a watermark detection API's result is a guide for initial screening, kept apart from the basis for judging authenticity?
- Do images your organization generates and releases carry a signature from you as the generator?
- Do you record which image was accepted on what basis, so it can be traced later?

## 5. Facts and timeline

- **Target**: Google DeepMind's SynthID, which embeds an invisible watermark in AI-generated images and judges via a detection API whether content is AI-generated; it covers images generated by Gemini.
- **Demonstrator**: Alosh Denny (independent researcher).
- **Form of disclosure**: Method, code, and results published on GitHub (`aloshdenny/reverse-SynthID`). A related `reverse-SynthID-text` targeting text detection also exists. Discussed in technical communities such as Hacker News.
- **Method**: No neural networks and no information held only by the provider. Using 123,000 Gemini-generated images, observes that SynthID encodes a fixed carrier frequency with constant phase, averages many images to extract the watermark's frequency pattern, and nullifies that frequency with a phase-shift attack (Denny's account).
- **Result**: About 91% of the watermark's energy removed; image quality almost intact (PSNR 43.5 dB / SSIM 0.997); reproduced on Gemini images at any resolution (results published by Denny).
- **Room for forgery**: Once the watermark's pattern can be extracted, the inverse of removal (injecting the watermark into non-AI images) can also hold on the same principle.
- **Nature**: Not an attack incident with a victim organization or damages, but a security-research demonstration of the limits of provenance marking.

### Timeline

- **2025-12-15**: Alosh Denny opens the GitHub repository `aloshdenny/reverse-SynthID` privately and starts development.
- **2026-03-05**: Publishes the method in the Medium article "How to Reverse SynthID (legally😉)."
- **Around 2026-04-11**: Discussion spreads on Hacker News (item 47709130).
- **From 2026-04**: A SynthID watermark bypass tool based on the method is reported in general media (MediaNama and others).
- **2026-08-02**: Scheduled start of the EU AI Act's watermarking obligation for AI-generated content (transparency requirement), as of this Brief's publication.

> This case is treated as a research / lab demonstration, not an intrusion by an attack. The scale of harm and operational impact are not exaggerated. The method and figures rest on what Alosh Denny published on GitHub and Medium, and each implementation's response status varies over time. What has not been disclosed does not mean there were no controls.

### Response after disclosure

- **Google DeepMind**: SynthID is offered as a watermarking technology for identifying AI-generated content. An official statement responding to this case could not be confirmed through official channels (blog, press release). Some secondary reporting (techbuzz.ai, stork.ai, and others) cites The Verge as the basis for stating that Google denied recognition of a SynthID compromise, but the cited article could not be confirmed for this Brief, so it is not treated as established.
- **Regulatory developments**: The EU AI Act's watermarking and transparency requirements for AI-generated content were scheduled to apply from 2026-08-02. The phase in which watermarking becomes a regulatory requirement overlaps with the phase in which its strip-ability and forge-ability were demonstrated, and the premise that "mandating watermarks secures provenance" is being questioned. Academic reports also indicate that only some AI image generators implement sufficient watermarking.
- **Academic developments**: Research on attacks against watermarks for generated content increased in 2025–2026. UnMarker (USENIX Security 2025) is regarded as the first general-purpose attack against defensive watermarks, and Warfare on arXiv (2023) also set out a general framework for watermark removal and forgery. This case is a concrete demonstration within that line.
