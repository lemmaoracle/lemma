## 1. Summary

The generated images mimic the texture of the shots commonly used in KYC capture, and even the capture data (EXIF), so they reach the receiving side in the same form as a photo of a genuine ID. Deepfake detection and image forensics stop many fraud attempts, but what they look for is traces of generation, which locks them in a race with the tools that generate. <strong>However genuine an ID image looks, whether the issuer actually issued that document cannot be confirmed from the image itself.</strong>

## 2. What got through

- **What got through**: Submission of a UK passport image generated by OnlyFake to the KYC check at the crypto exchange OKX. 404 Media reported that the image cleared KYC (2024-02-05).
- **What the receiving side gets**: In general, an image-based document check receives an image of the ID and its capture data. What it can review is appearance — template fit, texture, internal consistency. Whether the issuer issued the document is not contained in the image.
- **Operator claims**: An anonymous operator (self-styled "John Wick") claimed the service could clear KYC at major exchanges and crypto-friendly neobanks, and said a "neural network" generated the images. 404 Media said it could not verify the AI-use claim itself.
- **Not disclosed**: How many accounts were opened with generated images, and whether they were used for fraud or money laundering. At which stage OKX's review accepted the image.

## 3. Pre-execution check

The condition that holds whatever the route is this: before opening an account, the side accepting KYC checks whether the issuer issued the submitted ID, separately from how the image looks.

- **Who approves**: The ID's issuer (a government or issuing authority) or a verified identity provider. The issuer signs the person's attributes, and the receiving side checks that signature. It is not something the person submitting the ID can assert about themselves.
- **Where the check happens**: Before the account is opened. Check the issuer's signature, and that the document has not been revoked or expired. If it cannot be checked, KYC does not pass, however genuine the image looks. The receiving side gets only the attributes the requirement needs ("over 18," "not on a sanctions list"), not the original ID.
- **What remains**: A record of which attributes were checked against which issuer's signature when the account was opened, kept in a form that can be shown later in regulatory reporting or proceedings.

Detection is getting stronger. But detection that looks for traces of generation is a probabilistic judgment: what it cannot tell apart gets through, and it does not provide material to show later that an account was opened through legitimate identity verification. A related technique, swapping the face video through a virtual camera, is covered in [eKYC face-check bypass kits](https://lemma.frame00.com/critical/briefs/034-ekyc-liveness-bypass/) (Brief 034). Falsified personal qualifications appear in [the construction engineer qualification fraud](https://lemma.frame00.com/critical/briefs/019-construction-engineer-qualification-fraud/) (Brief 019), and the provenance of AI-generated content in [SynthID watermark reverse engineering](https://lemma.frame00.com/critical/briefs/011-synthid-watermark-reverse-engineering/) (Brief 011).

## 4. What Lemma does

**Lemma builds the pre-execution check into the side that accepts KYC.** It does not look for fake images; before an account is opened, it checks whether the person's attributes were issued with the issuer's signature, and receives only the attributes the requirement needs.

### Industries and operations to watch

- **Industry · Finance / FinTech**: Crypto exchanges, crypto-friendly neobanks, banks and other providers that open accounts online. An account that has cleared KYC is accepted as the precondition for later trades and transfers.
- **Operation · Account and permission management**: Account opening, where an ID image and a face video serve as the identity check.

### Current controls vs Lemma

The route in this case is column 1 (submitting an ID image). Column 2 is the step that WEF and others documented, defeating the face check with face swap or camera injection; whether that happened here has not been disclosed.

| Control | Submitting an ID image | Face check (selfie matching, liveness) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review of the image's appearance (template, texture, consistency) | Not effective | Not applicable |
| Checking capture data (EXIF) | Not effective | Not applicable |
| Deepfake detection, image forensics, injection detection | Partial | Partial |
| **Lemma (pre-execution check)** | Effective | Not applicable |

Reviewing appearance does not work, because the generated image mimics even the texture of typical KYC shots. Capture data does not work, because device, timestamp and location can be rewritten. Detection stops what it can tell apart, but it is a race with the generating side. Lemma checks the issuer's signature, not the image's appearance, before the account is opened. The face check lies outside Lemma's check.

Note: Lemma's check works only when the issuer issues the person's attributes with a signature and the accepting system builds that check in. Procedures that accept only images remain outside it. When someone else uses a genuine ID or a genuine credential, checking the signature cannot tell. The table compares what each control checks; it does not show whether this incident would have been stopped. Lemma does not detect or prevent intrusions.

### Checklist

Three items to confirm for providers that open accounts online

- Does your process avoid treating an ID image's capture data (device, timestamp, location) as evidence that it is genuine?
- Do you have a way to accept identity verification through issuer-signed digital credentials?
- Do you record which attributes were checked, and against what, when an account was opened, in a form you can show later?

## 5. Facts and timeline

- **Service**: OnlyFake. It generates ID images at USD 15 each, covering 26 countries including the US, Canada, the UK, Australia and EU member states. Buyers specify name, date of birth, address, expiry and signature; even the "shot lying on a carpet" texture common in KYC capture is reproduced (404 Media).
- **Capture data**: EXIF spoofing for capture device, timestamp and GPS location was reported to be supported (404 Media).
- **What was reported**: 404 Media reported that a UK passport image generated by OnlyFake cleared OKX's KYC. The case was also indexed in the OECD.AI incident registry.
- **Operator claims**: An anonymous operator (self-styled "John Wick") claimed the service could clear KYC at major exchanges and crypto-friendly neobanks. 404 Media said it could not verify the AI-use claim itself, while noting that the price point suggests automated generation.
- **Receiving side's response**: OKX said it saw the case as an industry-wide issue rather than a single-firm problem (Benzinga / Nasdaq).
- **Harm**: The number of real-world harms through accounts opened with generated images has not been disclosed.

### Timeline

- **2024-02-05**: 404 Media publishes the OnlyFake demonstration report, saying the image cleared OKX's KYC.
- **2024-02-05**: The case is indexed in the OECD.AI incident registry.
- **2024-02**: Industry press follows. OKX calls the case an industry-wide issue.
- **2025**: The World Economic Forum (WEF) and others document attack chains combining AI-generated documents, face swap and camera injection to defeat both document and face checks.
- **2025**: Identity-verification industry statistics report a 2,137% rise in deepfake-based fraud attempts over three years (Signicat).
- **H1 2025**: Deepfake-related fraud losses are reported to reach about USD 410 million in a half-year (aggregated by AInvest). Regulatory fines for AML/KYC violations are also reported to have risen sharply.

> Facts rest on 404 Media's report, the OECD.AI incident registry, follow-up reporting by Decrypt and Benzinga / Nasdaq, and industry statistics. This case is a press demonstration report, not a specific incident of realized harm through generated images. The AI-use claim is the operator's; 404 Media said it could not verify it. What has not been disclosed does not mean there were no controls.

### After disclosure

- **OKX**: Said it saw the case as an industry-wide issue.
- **Identity-verification vendors**: Have been strengthening deepfake and injection detection, while reporting that the race between generation and detection remains.
- **Policy**: A shift has begun toward identity verification built on issuer-signed digital credentials and disclosure of only the attributes a requirement needs, as seen in the EU's eIDAS 2.0 and EUDI Wallet.
