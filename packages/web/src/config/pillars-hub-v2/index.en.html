Trust Infrastructure

Trust infrastructure
for AI.

Provenance, AI decisions, authority, regulatory attributes. For each, a cryptographic proof.

Every time AI uses data, the questions follow: where did it come from, what did it look at to decide, on whose behalf did it act, and does it meet the regulations? Lemma trust infrastructure answers all four across four axes.

01 · Four axes

Each axis, one role.

Pillar 01 — Verifiable Origin
Verifiable Origin
Did this data really come from a legitimate issuer?

Bring the "where it came from" of business documents, API responses, IoT measurements and more into AI inputs as a tamper-proof cryptographic proof. A third party can independently verify the origin without touching the original.

your.provenance.v1 Learn more →
Pillar 02 — Verifiable AI
Verifiable AI
Did this model really decide on that input?

Record AI inputs and outputs, the model ID, and the applied policy as a cryptographic proof. The reasoning process is preserved in a form a third party can reproduce without accessing the original.

your.model.v1 Learn more →
Pillar 03 — Agent Authority Proof
Agent Authority Proof
Does this agent really hold the authority to act on someone's behalf?

Give an AI agent a cryptographic proof of "on whose behalf" and "within what scope" it may act — without handing over signing keys. Actions outside that scope are structurally rejected.

your.agent.v1 Learn more →
Pillar 04 — Regulatory Attribute Proof
Regulatory Attribute Proof
Does this attribute really meet the regulatory requirement?

Bring regulatory attributes such as KYC, age, and location into AI inputs in a provable form, without handing over the original. It implements the "independently verifiable" that masking and encryption can't reach.
